# Naming log

## Round 1 — 2026-11-13

**User's starting point.** "Power Agent" reads as Power Rangers and is taken; don't go that way. The
name should feel easy and approachable yet powerful. Liked the human-first-name trend; floated Jerry
and Jer.
**Directions.** Warm human first names (≈60 generated, 47 checked); everyday words for "small effort,
big effect" — levers, helpers, tailwind (≈50/24); plain two-word compounds for "easy yet powerful"
(≈60/33).
**What came out.** Every single-word name and every everyday word is taken on all of .com, .app and
.ai — 0 of 71 bare names had any of the three open (Nora, Dot, Edie had .app only). Prefixed forms of
Jerry (askjerry, meetjerry, heyjerry.app, jerryagent.com) are taken too. Jerry is also blocked by
jerry.ai, a 5M-user insurance app that calls itself an "AI agent"; Nora by a Shopify "AI Employee"
running agents on schedules plus several Nora companions; Dot by a "Dot — AI personal assistant".
Only two-word compounds still have open domains, mostly .app; smalllever.com is the single open .com.
**Where I looked for conflicts.** App Store search API (Jerry, Nora, Edie, Dot, Pocket Giant, Small
Lever, Easy Giant, Everyday Magic) and Exa web search (Jerry, Nora, Small Lever/Easy Giant/Pocket
Giant). Trademark registries not checked yet. Quiet Power, Plain Magic, Plus Hand, Long Lever and
Everyday Superpower have only the domain check.
**Best.** None strong yet. Easy Giant (.app likely-free, no app conflict) carries the brief's
"easy + strong" most directly; Pocket Giant the same, but pocketgiant.co ships Mac apps (notable).
**Proposed next.** A first name only works with an unusual-but-obvious name plus a modifier or a
non-.com TLD — ask whether the user accepts e.g. `<name>.app` or `get<name>.com`. Directions for round
2: (a) less common but one-spelling first names checked with `get`/`hey`/`.app` variants (Otis,
Wilbur, Rufus-type); (b) "giant" and "lever" compounds deepened (Gentle/Friendly/Kind Giant, Easy
Lever); (c) English words for a helper that has real power (sidekick-type nouns paired with a warm
adjective).

## Round 2 — 2026-11-13

**User on round 1.** `<name>.app` and `get<name>.com` are both fine. "Continue" — took the three
proposed directions.
**Directions.** Less common one-spelling first names with `get<name>.com` (48 new + 26 from round 1
re-checked with `get`); giant / lever / mighty compounds (15); helper nouns with a warm adjective (15).
Round-1 survivors re-checked with `get<name>.com`.
**What came out.** First names are closed even with the relaxed TLDs: none of 74 names had `.app`,
`.ai` or `get<name>.com` open — Otis, Wilbur, Mavis, Opal, Hazel, Wren included. The names direction
is done unless we coin a name. Compounds open up almost entirely: nearly every two-word compound has
`.app` and `get…com` likely-free. The constraint has moved from domains to conflicts and taste.
Sidekick compounds collide with monday sidekick, Tobit SideKick and Sidekick AI — dropped. Friendly
Giant collides with a crypto "Friendly Giant AI" agent and a Canadian kids' TV show. Mighty Kind and
Mighty Small belong to small beverage/food businesses (notable, unrelated). Kind Giant, Easy Lever and
Little Lever: nothing found.
**Where I looked for conflicts.** App Store search API (Gentle/Kind/Friendly Giant, Mighty
Easy/Kind/Small, Trusty Sidekick, Easy/Little Lever, "giant" in Mac apps) and Exa web search (the same
names in groups). Trademark registries still not checked. Porkbun confirmation was attempted for
kindgiant.app but Porkbun showed a CAPTCHA; nothing is registrar-confirmed.
**Best.** Kind Giant (kindgiant.app, getkindgiant.com likely-free; no conflict found) — the warmest
reading of "easy but strong". Gentle Giant (gentlegiant.app likely-free; band and moving company,
both unrelated) — a set phrase everyone spells right. Easy Giant (.app, get…com; only an Italian web
agency). Mighty Kind (.app, get…com; small unrelated businesses).
**Proposed next.** Ask the user whether "giant" is the right image at all — it is the only
direction that fits the brief and has room. If yes: deepen giant (Good Giant, Warm Giant, Calm Giant,
Giant in your pocket–style phrases) and run EUIPO/USPTO on the top two. If no: open a new image of
"small effort, big effect" — tailwind/current/tide, engine/motor, a coined name that sounds like an
English word.

## Round 3 — 2026-11-13

**User on round 2.** "Giant" is out — "sounds weak". Try names coined from scratch, or widen the
pool of real first names.
**Directions.** Coined first names that sound real, mostly CVCV ending in -o (44); coined names in
the user's Jerry/J vein (16); a broader pool of real names — Scandinavian, Italian, Japanese, Polish
nicknames (51). All checked on .com/.app/.ai and `get<name>.com`.
**What came out.** 34 of 111 had something open, almost all only `get<name>.com`. Seven had a bare
`.app`: Juva, Wilko, Jemmy, Jeppe, Jorry, Jerro, Franek (plus Ida, Tosia, Pavo). Most fail the
hearing test (J-names ending in -y/-ie, Jemmy = a burglar's crowbar) or carry a conflict: Ida — an
enterprise "Ida.Next" AI agent with schedules and memory (blocking); Tobo — "the AI teammate that does
the work", launched this month (blocking); Rodo = Polish GDPR; Osko = an Australian payments brand;
Wilko = a UK retailer and sounds like Wilco. Lesson: a coined name only gets a bare `.app` when it
is a bit unusual, and the J/-y/-ie endings the user likes are the ones with several spellings.
**Where I looked for conflicts.** App Store search API (Ida, Franek, Jerro, Juva, Wilko, Tobo, Nils,
Olek); Exa (Ida; Jerro/Juva/Wilko; Franek/Tobo). No trademark search; no registrar confirmation
(Porkbun showed a CAPTCHA in round 2).
**Best.** Jerro (jerro.app, getjerro.com; only a dissolved UK company) — closest to the user's own
Jerry, one risk: Jero/Gerro. Franek (franek.app, getfranek.com; Żabka's internal chatbot is also
called Franek) — warm for Poles, harder for English speakers. Juva (juva.app, getjuva.com; a
migraine app) — soft, neutral. Nils and Olek have only `get…com`.
**Proposed next.** Ask whether the name should feel Polish (Franek, Olek) or international (Jerro,
Juva). Then deepen the winner: Jerro-like coinages with the -o ending (Jerro, Benno, Tullo, Rallo),
or Polish nicknames that English speakers spell right (Kuba was taken; Felek, Tadek, Józek-like forms without Polish letters).
Run EUIPO/USPTO on the top two.

## Round 4 — 2026-11-13

**User on round 3.** Prefers an international feel; Slavic is fine if foreigners can say and spell
it easily. No verdict yet on Jerro, Franek or Juva.
**Directions.** Coined -o names in the Jerro vein (37); Slavic first names that foreigners can say
(49); Slavic words for strength, will, friend (7). Checked on .com/.app/.ai and `get<name>.com`.
**What came out.** The -o coinages are saturated: none had a bare `.app`, only `get…com` (Jarro,
Jero, Merro, Kerro, Lorro, Tullo). Slavic names do better: bare `.app` open for Radek, Bronek,
Bojan, Ratko, Kostya, Borya, Lyuba, Zdrav. Most of those fail for foreigners (Bojan read with an
English J; ly/zd clusters) or carry baggage (Ratko Mladić). Two survive: Radek and Bronek. Slavic
words for strength (Sila, Volya, Dobro) are all taken or hard to say.
**Where I looked for conflicts.** App Store search API (Radek, Bronek, Mirko, Janko, Jarro); Exa
(Radek; Bronek/Mirko/Janko). Only people and an inactive repo (mirko.ai, 2024) came up. Trademark
registries not checked; no registrar confirmation.
**Best.** Radek (radek.app, getradek.com; no product conflict, though a well-known OpenClaw
maintainer is called Radek) — short, Czech/Polish/Slovak, "rad" inside, English speakers may write
Radeck. Bronek (bronek.app, getbronek.com; no conflict) — "bro" makes it friendly; risk Bronnek.
Still standing from round 3: Jerro, Juva, Franek.
**Proposed next.** Put a finalist set in front of the user (Radek, Bronek, Jerro, Juva, Franek) and
ask which one feels right. For the one or two chosen: a hearing test with people who don't speak
Polish, EUIPO/USPTO search, Porkbun confirmation (the user may need to clear the CAPTCHA).

## Round 5 — 2026-11-13

**User on round 4.** Literal Polish names feel weird (Radek, Bronek, Franek, Olek rejected). Explore
unexpected directions, beyond first names.
**Directions.** Folklore household helpers that work while you sleep — brownies, hobs, tomte (25);
what a helper says — "on it", "sorted", "say the word" (33); strong, gentle working animals (16);
crew and household roles — squire, valet, sous (19); ease and leverage in motion — glide, coast,
torque (18). Checked on .com/.app/.ai and `get<name>.com`.
**What came out.** Single words from every direction are taken everywhere, `get` variants included
(Ox, Mule, Squire, Valet, Glide, Sorted, Brownie, Hob). The one exception is **overnight.app**. Helper
phrases keep `get…com` open (gethappyto.com, getsaytheword.com, getalreadydone.com) and sometimes a
bare `.app` (leaveittome.app). Folklore helpers are open but loaded with pop culture (Night Elf =
WoW, House Elf = Harry Potter, Kobold = KoboldAI). Lesson: what still has room are phrases and an
occasional everyday word whose meaning is close to the product but not yet a brand.
**Where I looked for conflicts.** App Store search API (Overnight, Happy To, Already Done, With
Pleasure, Good Elf, Goodfellow, Say the Word); Exa (Overnight as an AI agent; Happy To / Already
Done / Good Elf). No trademark search; no registrar confirmation.
**Best.** Overnight (overnight.app likely-free, could be premium; no AI brand of that name, but
"overnight agent" is a common phrase in the category, so it is weakly distinctive). Happy To
(gethappyto.com and gethappytoo.com likely-free; no conflict; the warmest of the lot, but sounds
like "happy too"). Say the Word (getsaytheword.com; only rhythm games). Goodfellow (getgoodfellow.com;
Robin Goodfellow, the helpful household sprite; heard as "Goodfellas").
**Proposed next.** Ask which of the three surviving ideas pulls: (a) the helper's reply ("Happy
To", "Say the Word"), (b) "while you sleep" / overnight change, (c) folklore helpers without pop
culture. Deepen the chosen one with 40+ variants, plus a hearing test and EUIPO/USPTO for the top two.

## Round 6 — 2026-11-13

**User on round 5.** No verdict on the round-5 names. New framing: the brand should rest on a mascot
that shows what the app can do and animates states like waiting for the agent — OpenAI's simple
abstract dots as the reference. The name can be a first name or anything else. Recorded in BRIEF.
**Directions.** Simple abstract shapes that animate — blob, bean, ember, orb (40); small but mighty
creatures — tardigrade, octopus, ant, hedgehog (35); short character names — Pip, Boo, Gizmo, Pogo
(25); everyday cute words for a small character — doodlebug, puffball, inkblot (40).
**What came out.** The mascot vocabulary is the most saturated so far: of 140, only 2 had a bare
`.app` beyond twee compounds — **nib.app** and **waterbear.app** (plus wigglebug.app, snugbug.app).
Everything short and round (Blob, Bean, Pip, Puff, Orb, Bubble, Pebble, Octo, Gizmo) is taken on all
three TLDs and `get…com`. Key finding for the brand, not just the name: **InkBot (inkbot.io), a
Windows desktop app with a team of AI agents, already uses animated ink-blot mascots** whose face and
motion show thinking/working/waiting — the exact idea. Puffle (puffle.ai) is an "AI teammate" too.
**Where I looked for conflicts.** App Store search API (Waterbear, Nib, Puffball, Inkblot, Wigglebug,
Fuzzball); Exa (Waterbear; Puffball/Inkblot/Wigglebug). No trademark search; no registrar
confirmation.
**Best.** Waterbear (waterbear.app likely-free; WaterBear is also a Dutch documentary-streaming
platform — notable, different space). The tardigrade is tiny, chubby and nearly indestructible: the
brief's "easy yet powerful" as a character, and simple to draw and animate. Nib (nib.app; an
Australian health insurer shares it) — a tiny drop-shaped mascot, but the word means little to most
people.
**Proposed next.** Separate the mascot from the name: pick the mascot concept first (tardigrade,
something abstract, an animal), then name the character — a name for the character is easier to
find than a word for the species (as with Clippy, Duo, Clawd). Ask the user whether the tardigrade
appeals; if so, generate names for it. Flag InkBot as a competitor whose mascot idea is close.

## Round 7 — 2026-11-13

**User on round 6.** Nib "looks very promising" — keep searching. Recorded as liked; what it shares
(one syllable, a tiny everyday object, the tip of a tool, easy to draw) noted in BRIEF as inferred.
**Directions.** Nib itself, deeper (variants and conflicts); one-syllable tool tips and small objects
like Nib (49); Nib variants (7); tiny marks, dots and bits (34). `.ai` re-checked at whois.nic.ai
where the script returned unknown.
**What came out.** Nib is blocked: **nib.gg is an AI agent product built like this one** — a daemon
as the agent runtime with desktop app, CLI and VS Code clients, crews and cloud sync. Around it:
nut.js's NIB CLI for desktop AI agents, usenib.dev (design context for coding agents), a Nib macOS
app for character illustrations, and nibby, nib insurance's AI assistant. Every Nib variant domain
except nib.app and nib.dev is taken; Porkbun listed nib.app without a price. One-syllable object
words are taken across the board, `get` variants included. Two-syllable "tiny thing" words still have
room: nibling.ai, smidgen.ai, pennino.app.
**Where I looked for conflicts.** Exa (Nib as AI/software; Nibling/Pennino/Smidgen); App Store search
API (Nibling, Pennino, Smidgen). Porkbun for nib.app (no price shown). No trademark search.
**Best.** Nibling (nibling.ai, getnibling.com; a sound-alike calorie app "Nibl AI") — keeps "nib",
"-ling" reads as a little creature, but heard as "nibbling". Pennino (pennino.app; nothing found) —
Italian for nib, soft and international; risk "Penino". Smidgen (smidgen.ai; smidgen.io sells
paywalls to AI agents — notable; several spellings).
**Proposed next.** Ask what drew the user to Nib — the short sound, the pen-tip meaning, or the
mascot shape — and search that axis: short coinages with the same shape (CVC, one obvious spelling),
writing-tool words from other languages (Pennino, Pluma), or tiny-thing words.

## Round 8 — 2026-11-13

**User on round 7.** What drew them to Nib was the short name with a punchy, catchy sound — not the
pen-tip meaning. Keep searching.
**Directions.** Short punchy coinages of three letters, consonant-vowel-consonant with one obvious
spelling (61); four-letter coinages that sound like a name (30). Survivors also checked on .dev,
.so, .co, .io; `.ai` confirmed at whois.nic.ai.
**What came out.** Three-letter names are gone on every TLD we use: 0 of 61 had a bare .com or .ai,
one had .app (tez.app). With `get…com` about a dozen stay open (getneb, getzib, getkib, gettiv…),
and .so/.dev have some (neb.so, zib.dev, zib.so, tez.so, tiv.dev). Four-letter name-like coinages
(Tavi, Zuri, Lumi, Novi, Kibo) are taken everywhere. Lesson: a three-letter name is possible only
as `get<name>.com` or on a secondary TLD such as .so or .dev — the TLD rule is now the deciding
constraint, not ideas.
**Where I looked for conflicts.** App Store search API (Zib, Neb, Tez, Tula, Rov); Exa (Zib/Neb/Tez as
AI or software). No trademark search; no registrar confirmation.
**Best.** Neb (getneb.com, heyneb.com, neb.so; no conflict found) — the closest to Nib in sound; an
old word for a beak. Zib (getzib.com, zib.dev, zib.so; Zib Digital, an Australian agency selling AI
agents — notable). Tez (tez.app, tez.so; a small text expander and an offline AI assistant use it,
and Google Pay was called Tez until 2018 — notable).
**Proposed next.** Ask the user whether .so or .dev is acceptable for a short name, or whether a
name with `get` is enough. Then generate a wider systematic set of three- and four-letter
coinages (several hundred, filtered for spelling) against `get…com` + .so + .dev, and run
EUIPO/USPTO on the best two.
